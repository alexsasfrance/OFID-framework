Mesoscopic Quantum Coherence Reconstructibility

Overview of the OFID framework

This overview presents the experimental perspective developed within the OFID framework, with emphasis on mesoscopic regimes in which the reconstructibility of quantum coherence may be probed operationally.

The central question is not whether coherence is lost, but whether it remains fully reconstructible under controlled conditions.

Experimental perspective

This experimental proposal focuses on mesoscopic regimes in which the reconstructibility of quantum coherence can be probed under controlled conditions, in connection with ongoing quantum decoherence experiments in mesoscopic systems.

The objective is not to replace standard decoherence theory, but to test whether a residual loss of reconstructibility may remain after known environmental mechanisms have been accounted for.

In this perspective, the relevant signature would be:

  • either an irreducible floor in coherence reconstructibility,
  • or a transition appearing under controlled parameter variation,
  • without attribution to known environmental decoherence channels.

Candidate platforms include mesoscopic interferometric systems, matter-wave approaches, and optomechanical configurations such as levitated nanoparticles.

A complementary phenomenological extension allowing such observations to be translated into quantitative constraints is developed in the Constraints text.

OFID-G: operational formulation

The OFID-G text provides the operational extension of the framework. It formulates in testable terms how one may investigate possible limits to reconstructibility while remaining within the standard formal structure of quantum theory.

Its role is to bridge the conceptual proposal and the experimental perspective, by identifying the type of signature that would be relevant and the conditions under which such a signature could be meaningfully interpreted.

Phenomenological constraints

The Constraints text introduces a minimal phenomenological framework designed to translate experimental outcomes into quantitative bounds on possible residual limitations to coherence reconstructibility.

Rather than introducing a specific dynamical model, it defines an operational residual quantity after independent calibration of known environmental decoherence channels, and expresses it in terms of a controllable effective parameter.

Within this framework, null experimental results can be interpreted either as upper bounds on a continuous residual contribution or as lower bounds on the location of a possible transition regime.

This provides a direct interface between the experimental perspective and standard methodologies of parameter estimation and sensitivity analysis in quantum experiments.

OFID: conceptual background

The conceptual framework of OFID introduces the idea that some degrees of freedom may remain physically real while being structurally inaccessible to local observers.

In this view, global evolution remains unitary, while local descriptions may encounter intrinsic limits of accessibility. The framework does not introduce new dynamical laws, but proposes a structural perspective on decoherence, measurement, and reconstructibility.

In this broader conceptual perspective, any observed limitation in reconstructibility would not be interpreted as a replacement of standard environmental decoherence, but rather as a possible completion of its effective scope.
