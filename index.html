Testing reconstructibility of quantum coherence in mesoscopic systems

Experimental page of the OFID framework

This page presents a concrete experimental framework aimed at probing a possible limit of quantum coherence reconstructibility beyond standard environmental decoherence, and potentially linked to gravitational constraints.

Unlike standard approaches, this test does not probe the loss of coherence, but the possibility of its complete reconstruction under controlled conditions.

Overview

This experimental proposal focuses on mesoscopic regimes in which the reconstructibility of quantum coherence can be probed under controlled conditions.

The central strategy is to independently calibrate known environmental decoherence channels, verify that coherence can be restored by acting on these channels, and then vary a controlled gravitational configuration while keeping the environment fixed.

In this setting, a key question is whether coherence remains fully reconstructible, or whether a residual limitation persists that cannot be reabsorbed into standard decoherence mechanisms.

In operational terms, the relevant signature would be the failure to fully restore coherence under a monotonic variation of gravitational parameters alone, despite prior validation that environmental decoherence can be independently controlled. Such a signature could take the form of either an irreducible floor in reconstructibility, or a transition appearing under controlled parameter variation.
